## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** RAG

**Description:**
DocuRAG's Phase 2 backend is a retrieval-augmented document Q&A system that generates streamed natural-language answers with grounded inline `[N]` citation markers. Knowledge workers, prosumers, and (per the repo's own test fixtures) people doing legal and financial document review — reading merger agreements, annual reports, contracts — upload their own PDFs and ask questions about them. Every factual claim in the answer must carry a clickable inline citation that resolves server-side to a specific `chunk_id`, `document_id`, `page`, and a *verbatim* supporting quote drawn from that chunk. "Good" is not a fluent answer — it is an answer where a domain expert can click any `[N]`, land on the exact passage, and confirm the cited text genuinely says what the answer claims it says. The LLM call site sits behind a thin provider abstraction so Groq can be swapped for Gemini/OpenRouter/Anthropic without touching the graph; "good" also means that swap never silently changes citation behavior.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. **Fabricated or non-verbatim supporting quote** — a `[N]` marker resolves to a "quote" that does not appear word-for-word in the cited chunk (LLM paraphrased, normalized whitespace, or invented it). The single highest-stakes failure: it manufactures false evidence a reviewer may act on.
2. **Citation misattribution (real chunk, wrong claim)** — the marker resolves to a real, retrieved chunk, but that chunk does not actually support the sentence it is attached to. The citation *looks* trustworthy and is therefore more dangerous than an obviously broken one.
3. **Confident citation when retrieval missed the answer** — the relevant passage was never retrieved, but the model still emits a `[N]` pointing at the closest-but-wrong chunk instead of abstaining. Grounding theater over an empty result.
4. **Source/marker misalignment** — `[N]` resolves to the wrong chunk due to off-by-one or reordering between context-build (`filtered_sources` numbering) and `done`-event emission. The whole answer can be subtly shifted by one source.
5. **Uncited claims presented as grounded** — substantive factual assertions in the answer carry no `[N]` at all, so the user cannot tell which statements are document-backed and which are model prior.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Legal / financial document review (knowledge-work document Q&A) — repo fixtures are a merger agreement contract and an annual report; the system is general-purpose BYO-PDF but its highest-stakes use context is contract, filing, and compliance review.

**User Population:** Individual knowledge workers and prosumers reading their own documents — including lawyers/paralegals reviewing contracts and agreements, analysts reading annual reports and financial filings, and researchers/compliance staff who must trace every assertion back to a source page. Desktop, single-user, BYO-documents in v1.

**Stakes Level:** High — users act on cited answers in legal and financial contexts where a misattributed or fabricated citation can drive a wrong contractual, investment, or compliance decision. Not Critical (no automated action, human always in the loop, reviewer can click through to verify) — but the entire product value collapses if citations cannot be trusted.

**Output Consequence:** A reviewer reads the answer, clicks a `[N]` badge, and relies on the cited page + quote to confirm a contract term, a financial figure, or a compliance fact — then makes or advises a decision. A wrong-but-confident citation doesn't just mislead; it manufactures false evidence that survives review because it *looks* sourced.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

| Dimension | Good (expert accepts) | Bad (expert flags) | Stakes | Source |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------------|--------|--------|
| Quote verbatim-ness | The supporting quote for each `[N]` appears word-for-word (modulo whitespace) in the cited chunk — a reviewer can Ctrl-F it on the page. | The quote is paraphrased, summarized, stitched from two passages, or absent — anything a reviewer cannot literally find on the cited page. | Critical | Stanford "Hallucination-Free?" legal RAG study; FACTUM citation-hallucination work. |
| Claim-to-source support (attribution faithfulness) | The cited chunk actually entails the sentence it is attached to — an NLI check or expert read confirms support for that specific claim. | The chunk is real and retrieved but does not support the claim ("real source that never said that") — citation misattribution. | Critical | "Correctness is not Faithfulness"; Stanford legal RAG taxonomy. |
| Citation coverage (recall) | Every substantive factual claim in the answer carries at least one `[N]`; the reviewer can audit every assertion. | Material claims — a dollar figure, an obligation, a date — are stated with no marker, indistinguishable from model prior. | High | Citation recall metric; REQ-grounded-citations "every claim has a clickable inline citation." |
| Citation precision (no spurious markers) | Every emitted `[N]` resolves to a real, relevant source; no decorative or padded citations. | Markers point at loosely-related or irrelevant chunks, or `[N]` exceeds the source count — precision degrades, trust erodes. | High | Citation precision/recall/F1 framework; D-03 (unresolvable markers). |
| Calibrated abstention on retrieval miss | When retrieval did not surface a passage that answers the question, the answer says so rather than citing the nearest wrong chunk. | The model emits a confident answer with `[N]` markers over chunks that don't contain the answer — grounding theater. | High | Stanford legal RAG study; FACTUM long-form RAG findings. |

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

- **Fabricated supporting quote** — the quote attached to `[N]` is not a real substring of the cited chunk (LLM paraphrase / whitespace normalization / invention). Directly motivates the Section 4 decision to derive quotes by deterministic post-hoc substring match rather than trusting an LLM-emitted quote.
- **Citation misattribution** — a *real, retrieved* chunk is cited for a claim it does not actually support. The most insidious mode because the badge resolves cleanly; only reading the chunk reveals the gap. Dominant category in the Stanford legal-RAG taxonomy alongside total fabrication.
- **Off-by-one / reordering source misalignment** — `[N]` resolves against `filtered_sources` positionally; any reordering between context-build and `done`-event emission shifts every citation. D-02's chunk_id-based *client* contract mitigates the frontend half, but server-side positional resolution remains the exposure.
- **Confident citation despite retrieval miss** — the reranked top-N never contained the answer, but the model cites the closest chunk anyway instead of abstaining. RAG reduces but does not eliminate this (Stanford: 17–33% hallucination even in production legal RAG tools).
- **Citation drift in marker format** — model emits `[Source 2]`, `[1, 2]`, or `[5]` against 4 sources; if the tolerant parser mis-normalizes or fails to strip, the user sees a dead or wrong badge. Addressed by D-01/D-03 but is the domain-specific manifestation to test against.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

No regulation directly governs this v1 deployment: it is a single-user, BYO-document reading tool with a human always in the loop, no automated decisioning, no advice issuance, no regulated-entity integration. The relevant *constraints* are contractual and reputational rather than statutory:
- **Privacy (LOCKED, project constraint):** user documents must not train any model — no-train API endpoints only (Anthropic, OpenAI zero-retention, Groq per ToS). This directly bounds which providers the Phase 2 seam may add.
- **Professional-responsibility exposure (downstream, not binding on v1):** if lawyers use the tool, fabricated/misattributed citations carry malpractice and court-sanction risk. This is *why* the citation rubric stakes are Critical, not a compliance requirement the phase must implement.
- If the product later targets regulated firms or issues anything resembling advice, FINRA/SEC recordkeeping and bar-association GenAI guidance would come into scope — flag for a future phase, out of scope for Phase 2.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** LangGraph (existing, pinned in `backend/requirements.txt`) + custom `LLMProvider` Protocol/factory seam

**Version:** As pinned in `backend/requirements.txt` (langgraph / langchain / langchain-community). No new framework dependency added.

**Rationale:**
The backend is already a LangGraph RAG graph with the LLM call isolated to `answer_node` and `stream_answer_with_graph`. Phase 2 only reroutes those two call sites through a new provider seam. CONTEXT.md D-05–D-08 locks a thin hand-rolled Protocol (`stream` / `generate` / `token_count`) + `get_provider()` factory keyed on `LLM_PROVIDER` — this is a structural seam, not a framework adoption. No new framework is warranted or permitted by the phase scope.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain `BaseChatModel` / `init_chat_model` as the provider abstraction | `langchain-community` is already a dependency so its chat-model interface is free, but it is heavier than the locked 3-method surface and makes per-provider tokenizer ownership (D-06) awkward. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No — the explicit purpose of the provider seam is to keep Groq swap-ready (Gemini/OpenRouter/Anthropic). No-train LLM endpoints only (privacy LOCKED).

### Entry Point Pattern
```python
# app/services/llm/base.py — the 3-method Protocol surface (D-06)
from typing import Protocol, Iterator, runtime_checkable

@runtime_checkable
class LLMProvider(Protocol):
    """Provider-agnostic LLM seam. Each provider owns its own tokenizer."""
    def stream(self, messages: list[dict]) -> Iterator[str]:
        """Yield raw content tokens. Provider raises its own SDK exceptions (D-08)."""
        ...
    def generate(self, messages: list[dict]) -> str:
        """Return a full completion string (non-streaming /chat path)."""
        ...
    def token_count(self, text: str) -> int:
        """Provider-owned tokenization. Groq/OpenAI -> cl100k_base; Gemini -> its own."""
        ...

# app/services/llm/groq_provider.py — wraps existing groq_client.py logic verbatim
class GroqProvider:
    def __init__(self) -> None:
        self._client = Groq(api_key=GROQ_API_KEY)
        self._enc = tiktoken.get_encoding("cl100k_base")  # singleton: built once per process

    def stream(self, messages: list[dict]) -> Iterator[str]:
        stream = self._client.chat.completions.create(
            model=GROQ_MODEL, messages=messages, max_tokens=MAX_TOKENS, stream=True,
        )
        for chunk in stream:
            token = chunk.choices[0].delta.content
            if token:
                yield token

    def generate(self, messages: list[dict]) -> str:
        completion = self._client.chat.completions.create(
            model=GROQ_MODEL, messages=messages, max_tokens=MAX_TOKENS,
        )
        return completion.choices[0].message.content or ""

    def token_count(self, text: str) -> int:
        return len(self._enc.encode(text))

# app/services/llm/factory.py — single config key switch (D-07)
_PROVIDER_CACHE: dict[str, LLMProvider] = {}

def get_provider() -> LLMProvider:
    """Explicit switch on LLM_PROVIDER. No 'guess from which keys are set'."""
    name = LLM_PROVIDER.lower()
    if name not in _PROVIDER_CACHE:
        if name == "groq":
            _PROVIDER_CACHE[name] = GroqProvider()
        # elif name == "gemini": _PROVIDER_CACHE[name] = GeminiProvider()  # v2 — new branch only
        else:
            raise ValueError(f"Unknown LLM_PROVIDER={name!r}")
    return _PROVIDER_CACHE[name]

# usage in langgraph_rag.py — replaces `from groq_client import stream_with_groq`
provider = get_provider()
for token in provider.stream(messages):
    ...  # tolerant parser sits HERE, between raw tokens and {token: ...} SSE events
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `typing.Protocol` (structural typing) | A duck-typed interface — a class satisfies it by having the right methods, no inheritance needed. `@runtime_checkable` enables `isinstance` checks. | Defining `LLMProvider` so Groq/Gemini/OpenRouter classes need zero shared base class — genuinely swap-ready without LangChain's `BaseChatModel` weight. |
| `get_provider()` factory | Maps the `LLM_PROVIDER` config string to a concrete provider instance, cached per process. | The single chokepoint the graph calls. Adding a provider = one new `elif` branch + one new class. |
| Per-provider tokenizer ownership | Each provider holds its own encoder (`GroqProvider` -> `cl100k_base`; future `GeminiProvider` -> Gemini's count API). `token_count` is on the Protocol, not global. | Token counts at the LLM boundary — context-budget checks before a call. **D-06**: `chunker/tokens.py` is a *separate* consumer — see Pitfall 1. |
| Tolerant streaming marker parser | A stateful filter consuming the provider's raw token iterator, emitting cleaned tokens, normalizing `[Source N]`/`[1, 2]` -> `[N]` and stripping unresolvable markers (D-01/D-03). | Wrapped around `provider.stream(...)` inside `stream_answer_with_graph`, before `{token: ...}` SSE events. Must buffer across token boundaries (Pitfall 2). |
| LangGraph node call site | `answer_node` (non-streaming) and `stream_answer_with_graph` (streaming) are the only two places the LLM is invoked — pure `state -> state` functions. | D-05 reroutes exactly these two through `get_provider()`. The rest of the graph is untouched. |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Two divergent tokenizers (D-06 — MUST reconcile in planning).** `chunker/tokens.py` builds its own module-level `cl100k_base` singleton; the new `GroqProvider.token_count` builds another. Both use `cl100k_base` *today* so they agree — but that is a coincidence, not a contract. **Recommended resolution:** the chunker keeps its own tiktoken path (chunking is an ingest-time, provider-agnostic concern and must stay stable across provider swaps); add a cross-reference comment in both files citing D-06. Do **not** route the chunker through `get_provider()`.
2. **Markers split across token boundaries.** A `[2]` marker can arrive as `"["` then `"2]"` in two separate stream chunks. A naive `re.sub` per token will never match. The parser must hold a small tail buffer — keep the last few chars unflushed until you know they are not the start of a marker.
3. **`runtime_checkable` only checks method *names*, not signatures.** `isinstance(obj, LLMProvider)` passes with wrong arguments. Treat the Protocol as a static-typing aid (run `mypy`/`pyright` in CI); do not rely on `isinstance` for correctness.
4. **Provider instance caching vs. the Groq client.** `Groq(api_key=...)` opens an HTTP connection pool. Build `GroqProvider` once (factory cache) — but not at *import* time the way `groq_client.py` does today (`_client = Groq(...)` at module scope crashes import if `GROQ_API_KEY` is unset, e.g. in tests). Construct lazily inside `get_provider()`.
5. **Groq SDK chunk shape is version-sensitive.** `chunk.choices[0].delta.content` is stable in groq 1.x but the SDK is pre-2.0 and fast-moving (1.2.0 installed, 1.3.0 out). Pin `groq==1.2.0`; treat an SDK bump as a code change requiring re-test of the streaming path.
6. **`generate` raising `HTTPException` leaks FastAPI into the seam.** Current `_call_groq` raises `fastapi.HTTPException(503)`. Per D-08 the *provider* should raise its SDK's native exception and let the call site translate — keep `HTTPException` out of `GroqProvider`.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**
- **Provider/model (v1):** Groq, `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` (current `GROQ_MODEL` default in `config.py`). Selected by `LLM_PROVIDER=groq` (D-07).
- **`max_tokens`:** `MAX_TOKENS` (default 4096) — passed explicitly on every call, already done in `groq_client.py`. Keep explicit (never unbounded — see 4b).
- **`temperature`:** not currently set; for grounded citation answers add `temperature=0.0–0.2` to the `create()` call to reduce marker drift and fabricated quotes. Surface as a constant in `GroqProvider` (or a `LLM_TEMPERATURE` config key).
- **New config key:** `LLM_PROVIDER = os.getenv("LLM_PROVIDER", "groq")` in `app/config.py`, flat-constant style matching existing keys.

**Core Pattern — streaming citation emission + tolerant parse:**
```python
# stream_answer_with_graph (langgraph_rag.py) — citation path
provider = get_provider()
parser = CitationStreamParser(filtered_sources)   # knows N -> chunk_id, positional (D-02)

try:
    for raw_token in provider.stream(messages):
        # parser buffers across boundaries, normalizes [Source N]/[1,2] -> [N],
        # strips unresolvable markers + logs them (D-01/D-03)
        for clean_token in parser.feed(raw_token):
            yield json.dumps({"token": clean_token})
    for clean_token in parser.flush():            # emit any buffered tail
        yield json.dumps({"token": clean_token})
except Exception as e:                            # D-08 — caller handles, as today
    print(f"stream error: {type(e).__name__}: {e}")
    yield json.dumps({"token": " [answer temporarily unavailable]"})

# D-04 — final done event carries enriched sources[] + marker->chunk_id map
yield json.dumps({
    "done": True,
    "sources": parser.enriched_sources(),         # [{chunk_id, page, quote, ...}]
    "marker_map": parser.marker_to_chunk_id(),    # {"1": "chunk_abc", ...}
})
```

**Tolerant parser — regex vs state machine:** Recommended is a **buffered-regex hybrid**. Accumulate tokens into a `str` buffer; after each `feed()` run one normalization regex over the buffer: `\[\s*(?:Source\s*)?(\d+(?:\s*,\s*\d+)*)\s*\]` — captures `[N]`, `[Source N]`, `[1, 2]`. For a complete match, resolve each `N` positionally against `filtered_sources`: resolvable -> rewrite to canonical `[N]`; unresolvable -> drop + `print()` log (D-03). **Only flush buffer content up to the last position that cannot be the prefix of an in-progress marker** — hold back a trailing `[`, `[Sou`, `[1,` until the next token completes or disproves it. A pure state machine works but is more code for no accuracy gain; the marker grammar is tiny. `flush()` at stream end runs the regex one final time and emits everything remaining (an unterminated `[` is then literal text).

**Supporting-quote extraction (Claude's discretion — CONTEXT.md; SC2 requires a verbatim substring per cited chunk):**

| Strategy | Latency | Cost | Accuracy / verbatim guarantee | Verdict |
|----------|---------|------|-------------------------------|---------|
| (a) LLM emits quote structured | Free — same call | Free — same call | Low verbatim guarantee: LLMs paraphrase/normalize whitespace; exact `in` check frequently fails. Needs a verify-or-fallback step anyway. | Use only as a *hint* feeding (b). |
| (b) Post-hoc fuzzy substring match against the cited chunk's text (`rapidfuzz`/`difflib`), snapped to the chunk's actual char span | ~1–5 ms per marker, in-process | Zero LLM cost | High — the returned quote is *by construction* a real substring of the chunk (SC2 satisfied deterministically). | **Recommended for v1.** Deterministic, cheap, no extra LLM dependency. |
| (c) Cheap second LLM pass | +1 round-trip per message/marker | Doubles call volume; Groq 429s already bit the project in Phase 1 UAT | High *if* still verified as substring — else same paraphrase risk as (a) | Reject for v1 — worst latency/cost. |

**Recommendation: Strategy (b)** — post-hoc fuzzy substring match in `app/services/citations/quotes.py`: input = `(chunk_text, answer_span_near_marker)`, output = a `(start, end)` char span into `chunk_text`. It is the only option that makes "verbatim supporting quote substring" a *guarantee*, adds no LLM cost/latency, and does not aggravate the known Groq rate-limit problem. **Grounding-strictness corollary** (also Claude's discretion): if the best fuzzy match scores below a threshold, the quote is genuinely unsupported — for v1, fall back to the chunk's leading sentence and `print()`-log the low score (consistent with D-03's thin-logging posture; hard rejection of unsupported markers is a v2 eval-surface candidate, not this phase).

**Tool Use:** None. No function-calling / tool APIs in this phase — the LLM produces a text answer with inline `[N]` markers only. The "tools" are internal: the tolerant parser and the fuzzy-quote matcher, both pure Python.

**State Management:** Unchanged. LangGraph nodes stay pure `state -> state`; `filtered_sources` already flows through graph state (built by `expand_to_parents_node`, numbered `[Source 1..N]`). The parser is constructed *per request* from `filtered_sources` — it holds only transient streaming state (buffer + marker map), never persisted. The per-message `sources[]` contract (DEC-per-message-sources) is satisfied by the enriched `done`-event payload (D-04).

**Context Window Strategy:** No change to retrieval. `RAG_TOP_K=30` -> reranker -> `RERANKER_TOP_N=5` chunks is the existing budget and stays. The provider seam *enables* a future per-provider context check via `provider.token_count(...)` on the assembled prompt before calling — recommended as a guard (log a warning if prompt tokens approach the model limit) but not required to ship Phase 2. Citation markers add only a handful of tokens to output; `MAX_TOKENS=4096` is ample.

## 4b. AI Systems Best Practices

> Written by `gsd-ai-researcher`. Cross-cutting patterns every developer building AI systems needs — independent of framework choice.

### Structured Outputs with Pydantic

The Phase 2 *answer* is streamed free text with inline `[N]` markers — it is **not** a structured-output call, and forcing the streamed answer through a Pydantic schema would break token-by-token UX. Pydantic instead validates the **assembled `done`-event payload** (D-04), which is the real contract the frontend depends on:

```python
# app/models/schemas.py — extends the existing Source model (adds chunk_id + quote per D-04/SC3)
from pydantic import BaseModel, field_validator

class Source(BaseModel):
    chunk_id: str
    document_id: str
    page: int
    quote: str            # verbatim substring of the cited chunk (SC2)
    text: str
    filename: str
    distance: float

class DoneEvent(BaseModel):
    done: bool = True
    sources: list[Source]
    marker_map: dict[str, str]   # "N" (as emitted) -> chunk_id (D-02)

    @field_validator("marker_map")
    @classmethod
    def markers_resolve_to_known_chunks(cls, v, info):
        known = {s.chunk_id for s in info.data.get("sources", [])}
        for marker, cid in v.items():
            if cid not in known:
                raise ValueError(f"marker {marker} -> unknown chunk_id {cid}")
        return v
```
- **How it integrates:** no framework `.with_structured_output()` or `instructor` — this is plain Pydantic validation of a payload *we* assemble from the parser, before `json.dumps`. The LLM never sees the schema.
- **Retry logic:** there is nothing to "retry" against the LLM — a validation failure here means a *parser bug* (e.g. a marker mapped to a chunk not in `sources[]`). On `ValidationError`: do **not** retry; `print()`-log it (D-03 thin-logging) and ship the `done` event with the offending marker dropped from `marker_map` — degrade gracefully rather than 500 the stream. The optional structured-quote *hint* from strategy (a), if used, IS validated-or-discarded — but the source of truth is the deterministic fuzzy match (b), so a bad hint never blocks.

### Async-First Design

- **Current reality:** the LangGraph graph and `groq_client.py` are **synchronous** — `stream_with_groq` is a sync generator, FastAPI runs it via `StreamingResponse` over a sync iterator. `GroqProvider` should match: keep `stream()`/`generate()` sync to land Phase 2 as a pure seam refactor with zero concurrency-model change.
- **The seam stays async-ready:** `groq` ships `AsyncGroq`. A future `stream()` could become `AsyncIterator[str]` — but changing sync->async ripples through `langgraph_rag.py` and `routes_chat.py`, so do it as its own phase, not smuggled into this one.
- **The one common mistake:** calling `asyncio.run()` inside an already-running event loop (FastAPI's). FastAPI endpoints are *already* async — never wrap a provider call in `asyncio.run()` from a route handler. Sync provider methods called from an `async def` route should be offloaded with `await run_in_threadpool(...)` if they block (the existing streaming path handles this via `StreamingResponse`).
- **Stream vs. await:** stream the *answer* (token UX is the whole point — D-01 emits markers during generation). Use the non-streaming `generate()` only for the `/chat` non-streaming path, and for any future structured sub-call where you need the *complete* output before validating.

### Prompt Engineering Discipline

- **System vs. user separation:** the citation instruction ("cite supporting context as compact `[N]` markers, where `N` is the source number; cite only sources that directly support the claim; do not invent source numbers") belongs in the **system** prompt — it is a stable behavioural contract. The retrieved context blocks (`[Source 1]..[Source N]`, already assembled by `expand_to_parents_node`) and the user question go in the **user** message. This is a change to `_build_answer_messages`, which today asks for `[Source N]` — D-01 switches the *ask* to compact `[N]`.
- **Few-shot:** inline 1–2 tiny static examples in the system prompt showing a sentence followed by `[2]` and the no-fabrication rule. Do **not** dynamically retrieve few-shot examples — the citation grammar is fixed and trivial.
- **Token budget:** `max_tokens` is already passed explicitly (`MAX_TOKENS=4096`) — keep it that way in `GroqProvider`; never call `create()` without it. Belt-and-suspenders with the tolerant parser: prompt for `[N]` *and* normalize drift, because even a well-prompted model emits `[Source 2]` or `[1, 2]` occasionally (D-01).

### Context Window Management

RAG-type system. Retrieval budget is fixed and pre-LLM: `RAG_TOP_K=30` candidates -> cross-encoder reranker -> `RERANKER_TOP_N=5` chunks into the prompt. With parent-expansion (`expand_to_parents_node`) those 5 become parent blocks — still comfortably inside the Llama 3.3 70B context window at `PARENT_CHUNK_TOKENS=1024` each (~5K context tokens + system prompt). No summarisation or truncation needed for Phase 2. The provider seam's `token_count` makes a *future* pre-call guard cheap: `if provider.token_count(assembled_prompt) > LIMIT: log.warning(...)` — recommended as an observability line, not a hard requirement to ship. Output side: citation markers cost ~3–5 tokens each; negligible against `MAX_TOKENS`.

### Cost and Latency Budget

- **Per-message cost:** one Groq chat-completion call per user message (streaming). Groq's pricing for `llama-3.3-70b-versatile` is low (sub-cent per typical RAG turn at ~5K input + few-hundred output tokens). **Strategy (b) for quotes adds zero LLM cost** — fuzzy matching is in-process Python (~1–5 ms per marker). This is the decisive cost argument against quote-strategy (c), which would *double* call volume.
- **Latency:** dominated by the single streaming call (Groq is fast — that is the point of the choice). The tolerant parser adds microseconds per token; the fuzzy-quote pass runs once at `done`-event assembly, low-single-digit milliseconds total. No second round-trip.
- **Rate limits:** Groq 429s bit the project in Phase 1 UAT. v1 mitigation is *manual* `LLM_PROVIDER` switching, not auto-fallback (D-08; INFRA-V2-01 deferred). The seam being genuinely swap-ready IS the cost/latency contingency plan.
- **Caching / sub-task routing:** none needed in Phase 2. There is exactly one LLM call per message and no classification/routing sub-tasks to offload to a cheaper model. If a future phase adds query rewriting or relevance grading, route those through a smaller/cheaper model via the same `get_provider()` seam (e.g. a `LLM_PROVIDER_FAST` key) — the abstraction already supports it.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

### Dimensions

These map directly to Section 1b's "What Domain Experts Evaluate Against" table and Section 1's Critical Failure Modes. The Section 4 decision to derive quotes by **deterministic post-hoc fuzzy substring match** is what makes the two highest-stakes dimensions code-checkable rather than LLM-judged — lean on that.

| Dimension | Rubric (Pass/Fail) | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------------------|----------------------|----------|
| **Quote verbatim-ness** (FM-1) | **PASS:** every `[N]`'s supporting `quote` is found word-for-word (modulo whitespace normalization) in the cited chunk's `text`. **FAIL:** quote is paraphrased, stitched, or absent — a reviewer cannot Ctrl-F it on the page. | **Code** — `quotes.py` returns a char span by construction; assert the span re-slices to the quote and the quote is a substring of `chunk_text`. | **Critical** |
| **Marker → source resolution** (FM-4) | **PASS:** every emitted `[N]` appears in `marker_map`; every `marker_map` value is a `chunk_id` in `done.sources[]`; positional lookup matches the chunk the span was generated against (no off-by-one). **FAIL:** `[N]` resolves to wrong/missing chunk. | **Code** — `DoneEvent` Pydantic validator + an ordering test: shuffle `filtered_sources`, assert `marker_map` still resolves by `chunk_id`. | **Critical** |
| **`done`-event schema compliance** (SC2, SC3) | **PASS:** `done` event validates against `DoneEvent`; every `Source` carries non-empty `chunk_id`, `document_id`, `page` (int ≥ 1), `quote`. **FAIL:** missing field, wrong type, or unresolvable marker leaked into user-facing text. | **Code** — Pydantic `DoneEvent.model_validate()` in pytest against streamed fixture Q&A output. | **Critical** |
| **`document_ids` filter regression-free** (SC5) | **PASS:** a query with `document_ids=[doc_A]` only cites chunks whose `document_id == doc_A`. **FAIL:** any cited chunk leaks from a document outside the filter. | **Code** — extends the existing Phase 1 `document_ids` regression test to also assert citation provenance. | **Critical** |
| **Claim-to-source support / faithfulness** (FM-2) | **PASS:** the cited chunk actually entails the sentence the `[N]` is attached to. **FAIL:** real, retrieved chunk cited for a claim it does not support. | **LLM Judge** (NLI-style: claim + chunk → supported/unsupported), **calibrated against Human** — lawyer/analyst adjudicate edge cases; do not trust the judge until ≥ 0.7 correlation with expert labels. | **Critical** |
| **Calibrated abstention on retrieval miss** (FM-3) | **PASS:** on a question whose answer is not in the retrieved top-N, the answer says so rather than emitting confident `[N]` markers. **FAIL:** confident answer with citations over chunks that don't contain the answer. | **LLM Judge** + **Human** — requires adversarial "answer-not-in-doc" examples; senior practitioner owns the threshold. | **High** |
| **Citation coverage / recall** (FM-5) | **PASS:** every substantive factual claim carries ≥ 1 `[N]`. **FAIL:** material claims stated with no marker. | **LLM Judge** (claim extraction → marker presence), spot-checked by **Human**. | **High** |
| **Citation precision / no spurious markers** (FM-4) | **PASS:** every emitted `[N]` is resolvable and points at a relevant chunk; no unresolvable marker reaches the user. **FAIL:** markers point at loosely-related chunks. | **Code** for resolvability (parser strip-rate, `DoneEvent` validator) + **LLM Judge** for relevance. | **High** |
| **Provider-swap citation invariance** (SC4) | **PASS:** the citation contract holds identically when `LLM_PROVIDER` changes. **FAIL:** swapping providers changes marker format, breaks resolution, or degrades quote extraction. | **Code** — run the citation eval suite against a stub/fake `LLMProvider` (deterministic scripted output) to prove the non-LLM contract is provider-independent. | **Medium** |

### Eval Tooling

**Primary Tool:** **pytest** (existing suite) + **RAGAS** (faithfulness / answer-relevance only, for the semantic dimensions) — **no Phoenix, no platform.**

**Phoenix override — justification:** The workflow default is Arize Phoenix, but it is declined for Phase 2. The logging posture is deliberately thin (D-03: a `print()` placeholder; structured query logging is deferred to **EVAL-V2-02 / v2**), and this is a **single-user desktop tool** with no production traffic to trace. Standing up a Phoenix server + OpenTelemetry instrumentation is disproportionate, and the four Critical dimensions are *deterministic code checks*, not trace-analysis problems. The right-sized choice is the existing pytest harness (Phase 1 already built a parametrized harness over `fixtures/pdfs/`), with RAGAS pulled in only for the semantic dimensions. Phoenix/Langfuse becomes correct when EVAL-V2-02 lands real query logging.

**Setup:**

pip install ragas==0.2.*          # add to backend/requirements-dev.txt

### Reference Dataset

**Size:** 16 Q&A pairs to start (≥ 10 minimum; expand from real failures). Stored as `backend/eval/citations_golden.jsonl`.

**Composition:** built on the **real PDF fixtures already in `backend/tests/fixtures/pdfs/`** — the merger agreement contract and the annual report. Per-record schema: `{question, document_id, expected_pages: [int], expected_verbatim_span: str, answerable: bool, category}`.
- **6 critical-path / answerable** — contract-term and financial-figure lookups whose answer sits cleanly in one chunk. Exercises verbatim-ness + marker resolution.
- **3 table-derived** — figures that live in tables in the annual report (citations to table rows are a known-fragile path). Financial analyst calibrates what counts as a supported numeric claim.
- **3 multi-claim answers** — answers making ≥ 2 distinct factual assertions, each needing its own `[N]`. Exercises citation coverage/recall.
- **3 adversarial "answer-not-in-doc"** (`answerable: false`) — plausible questions whose answer is absent from the PDF. Exercises calibrated abstention.
- **1 marker-drift stress** — a question expected to elicit `[Source 2]` / `[1, 2]` drift, to confirm the tolerant parser normalizes to canonical `[N]`.

**Labeling:** by the Section 1b domain expert roles. **Contracts lawyer / paralegal** authors and labels the merger-agreement pairs and adjudicates claim-to-source support on edge cases. **Financial analyst** authors and labels the annual-report pairs, especially table-derived figures. **Senior practitioner / product owner** sets the verbatim-ness and faithfulness pass thresholds and calibrates the LLM judge against their own judgments before it is trusted. **Backend engineer** owns the harness and JSONL format. Build the dataset *concurrently with implementation*, not after.

## 6. Guardrails

Proportionate to a **v1 single-user desktop tool** — no automated decisioning, human always in the loop. "Guardrails" here are in-request correctness checks already implied by D-01/D-03/D-04, not new infra.

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Unresolvable-marker strip (D-01/D-03) | Tolerant parser sees a `[N]` with no matching source in `filtered_sources` (e.g. `[5]` against 4 sources). | **Block** — strip the marker from the user-facing stream so no dead badge is shown; `print()`-log the occurrence. |
| `done`-event schema validation (D-04 / Section 4b) | Assembled `DoneEvent` fails Pydantic validation — e.g. `marker_map` references a `chunk_id` absent from `sources[]`. | **Flag + degrade** — do NOT retry, do NOT 500 the stream; drop the offending marker from `marker_map`, `print()`-log, ship the rest. |
| Quote-not-verbatim fallback (Section 4, Strategy b corollary) | Best fuzzy substring match for a marker scores below threshold — quote is genuinely unsupported. | **Flag** — fall back to the cited chunk's leading sentence and `print()`-log the low score. Hard rejection is a v2 candidate. |
| `document_ids` provenance check (SC5) | Any cited chunk's `document_id` falls outside the request's `document_ids` filter. | **Block** — retrieval-filter regression; fail loudly in tests. Should never reach a user. |

### Offline (Flywheel)

The flywheel in v1 is deliberately minimal — the `print()` log is the only signal source, and **structured query logging is explicitly deferred to EVAL-V2-02 (v2)**. Do not build a logging pipeline here.

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Marker strip rate (unresolvable `[N]` per answer) | Scan `print()` logs after manual/UAT sessions; no automated collection in v1. | Rising strip rate → prompt-engineering pass on the `[N]` instruction, or tolerant-parser bug. |
| Low-score fuzzy-quote-match rate | `print()`-logged low scores reviewed by the backend engineer, sampled to lawyer/analyst for adjudication. | Persistent low scores → revisit quote-extraction threshold or chunk granularity. |
| Faithfulness / abstention scores on the golden dataset | Run `test_citations_semantic.py` against the 16-pair dataset nightly/pre-merge. | Score drop → senior practitioner reviews; recalibrate judge or fix prompt/retrieval. |
| Provider-swap drift | Re-run the full citation eval suite whenever `LLM_PROVIDER` changes or a provider SDK is bumped. | Any contract divergence between providers → release blocker. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing Tool:** **None in v1 — `print()` to stdout/server logs only (D-03).** This is a single-user desktop tool with no production traffic; a tracing platform (Phoenix / Langfuse) is unwarranted. The hooks are deliberately thin so EVAL-V2-02 can replace the `print()` calls with structured `query_log.jsonl` logging without re-architecting. **Explicitly deferred to v2:** structured query logging, `/admin/queries` endpoint, thumbs-up/down capture (EVAL-V2-02); any trace/observability platform.

**Key Metrics to Track** (via `print()` log inspection in v1; promoted to structured metrics in EVAL-V2-02):
1. **Unresolvable-marker strip count** per answer — D-03's core observability line.
2. **Low-score fuzzy-quote-match count** per answer — Section 4 Strategy (b) corollary.
3. **`DoneEvent` validation failures** — each one is a parser bug.
4. **Stream-error fallbacks** — occurrences of the `[answer temporarily unavailable]` token (D-08), the v1 proxy for Groq 429s.
5. **`token_count` vs. context-limit margin** — optional pre-call `log.warning` if assembled prompt approaches the model limit.

**Alert Thresholds:** No paging in v1 — there is no on-call for a single-user desktop tool. The practical threshold is **review-driven**: if a UAT or dev session's logs show *any* `DoneEvent` validation failure (parser bug — always investigate) or a strip rate above ~1 marker per answer, treat it as a release blocker before Phase 3. Repeated `[answer temporarily unavailable]` fallbacks → manually switch `LLM_PROVIDER` (the v1 mitigation; auto-fallback is INFRA-V2-01, v2).

**Smart Sampling Strategy:** In v1, "sampling" = the backend engineer scans the `print()` log after a UAT/dev session and forwards concerning interactions to the domain experts. Signal-based filters to weight toward: (a) answers with any stripped marker, (b) answers with a low fuzzy-quote score, (c) any `DoneEvent` validation failure, (d) any `document_ids`-filter anomaly. The lawyer/analyst adjudicate claim-to-source faithfulness on the sampled set; the senior practitioner recalibrates thresholds. When EVAL-V2-02 lands, this manual scan becomes an automated signal-weighted sampler over `query_log.jsonl`.
